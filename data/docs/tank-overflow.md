# Tank Overflow — Troubleshooting Guide

## Common in Indian homes & apartments

Roof tanks overflow when the pump does not stop after the tank is full. Wasted water, wet terraces, and neighbour complaints are common — especially where float switches fail or timers are not set.

## Symptoms

- Water spills from the tank **daily**, often at night or early morning.
- Pump runs **long after** you hear the tank “full” sound.
- Terrace or walls stay **damp**; water stains on adjacent flats.
- Electricity bill rises from **extra run hours**.

## Likely causes

1. **Faulty float switch** — Stuck “on”, tangled, or set too high.
2. **No auto-cut / timer** — Pump runs until someone switches off manually.
3. **Wrong float placement** — Installed too high or in turbulent water zone.
4. **Leaking foot valve** — Water drains back; pump cycles repeatedly.
5. **Timer set too long** — More minutes than needed to fill the tank.
6. **Multiple tanks, one pump** — Valves left open on empty tanks.
7. **High incoming pressure** — Fills faster than expected vs timer setting.

## Quick checks

- Watch the tank while the pump runs — does it **stop at full level**?
- Lift or move the float gently (power off first if wiring is nearby) — does it move freely?
- Note **how many minutes** the pump runs for a typical fill.
- Check if overflow happens **only when nobody is home** (timer issue).
- Inspect terrace for **blocked overflow pipe**.

## Fixes

- Adjust or replace the **float switch**; keep wiring dry and supported.
- Set a **timer** to slightly less than your usual fill time; fine-tune over a week.
- Repair **foot valve / non-return valve** if pump restarts soon after stopping.
- Close valves to **unused tanks** on the same line.
- Clear **overflow outlet** so you notice overflow early.

## Prevention tips

- Combine **float + timer** (timer as backup if float fails).
- Review settings after **monsoon** or when family size changes.
- Monthly test: run pump once and confirm **auto-stop at full**.
- Mark “normal fill time” on the timer for the next person who adjusts it.

## When to call a technician

- Sparks, burnt smell, or water near electrical junction boxes on the terrace.
- Repeated overflow after float and timer were replaced correctly.
- Rewiring needed for **3-phase** pump panels.

## Eshwar recommendation

An **Eshwar auto-cut timer** set to your tank’s fill time stops the pump even if the float sticks — useful as a daily backup in apartments and farmhouses. Pair with a good float for best results.

## Safety

Do not handle live float wires with wet hands. Keep children away from open tanks and slippery terrace edges during overflow.
