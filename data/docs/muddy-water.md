# Muddy Water — Troubleshooting Guide

## Common in borewells & open sources

Brown or muddy water at taps usually means sediment, collapsed bore walls, seasonal aquifer change, or rust from old pipes — not always a “dirty tank” alone.

## Symptoms

- Water **brown, yellow, or cloudy** right after pump starts.
- Clears slightly after **running several minutes**, then mud again next start.
- Grit in **taps and filters**; toilet stains.
- More mud after **heavy rain** or first run of the day.

## Likely causes

1. **Bore silt / fine sand** — Aquifer carries sediment when pumped hard.
2. **Dry or stressed bore** — Pulling mud from bottom zone.
3. **Open or damaged casing screen** — Soil entry into bore.
4. **Rust in GI pipelines** — Red-brown water, not true mud.
5. **Unclean roof tank** — Algae, dust, or dead leaves after cleaning gap.
6. **Construction nearby** — Vibration unsettles bore or tank sediment.
7. **Wrong pump flow rate** — Too high velocity pulls fines.

## Quick checks

- Fill a **clear glass** at start vs after 5–10 minutes run.
- Note if mud appears **only hot season** or after rains.
- Inspect **tank lid** — birds, dust, open mesh.
- Check **filter housings** for brown sludge.
- Compare **bore water vs municipal** line if dual supply.

## Fixes

- Run pump to **stable clear flow**, then use for storage (avoid first surge for drinking).
- Install or service **sediment filter** and clean regularly.
- **Reduce pumping hours** if bore is stressed; ask dealer about yield.
- **Clean roof tank** properly; seal lid and overflow.
- Replace **rusted GI sections** in critical lines over time.

## Prevention tips

- **Slow start** after long idle; don’t hammer dry bore.
- Flush lines after **tank cleaning** before household use.
- Test drinking water with a **lab kit** if colour persists — don’t guess safety.
- Keep **strainer at suction** per pump manual.

## When to call a technician

- Sudden **black, oily, or chemical smell** water — stop use; possible contamination.
- Bore yield collapsed with constant mud — may need **camera survey / flushing**.
- Need to lower pump or change **stages** — specialist job.

## Eshwar recommendation

Controlled **run time** with an Eshwar auto-cut timer avoids long aggressive pumping when the bore is already stressed — helps sediment settle between cycles.

## Safety

Do not drink cloudy water without testing. Boiling alone may not remove chemicals or heavy sediment. Infants and elders should use known-safe water until cleared.
