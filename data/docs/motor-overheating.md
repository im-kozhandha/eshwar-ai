# Motor Overheating — Troubleshooting Guide

## Common in Indian homes & farms

Submersible and monobloc motors often overheat when pumps run too long, voltage is unstable, or the borewell/water path is blocked. Heat damages winding insulation and shortens motor life.

## Likely causes

1. **Low voltage or voltage fluctuation** — Motor draws higher current; body gets hot.
2. **Running dry** — No water flow; heat builds in the motor/pump assembly.
3. **Blocked suction or foot valve** — Pump works harder; overload and heat.
4. **Wrong HP for depth/yield** — Undersized motor runs continuously at high load.
5. **Poor ventilation (surface motors)** — Dust, cover, or cramped pump room traps heat.
6. **Worn bearings or misalignment** — Friction and vibration increase temperature.
7. **Frequent start–stop** — High inrush current; thermal stress over time.
8. **Single-phasing (3-phase)** — One phase missing; motor hums, heats, may burn.

## Simple checks (safe, non-electrical first)

- Confirm **water is actually flowing** at the outlet when the motor runs.
- Check **tank level** and whether the pump should still be running (overflow timers help).
- Listen for **unusual noise** (grinding = bearing; clicking = electrical issue).
- For surface setups, ensure **air space around the motor** is not blocked.
- Note if overheating happens **only in summer evenings** (often voltage dip).

## Simple fixes

- **Stop the motor** if the body is too hot to touch or you smell burning — allow cooling before restart.
- Fix **dry-run**: ensure borewell has water; repair foot valve/suction leaks.
- Clean **strainer and pipes** if flow is weak.
- Avoid **continuous 24×7** operation unless designed; use a timer or float-based auto-cut.
- Stabilize supply where possible (voltage stabilizer for sensitive sites — consult a local electrician).
- Service **bearings and alignment** yearly for heavy-use agricultural motors.

## Prevention tips

- Use a **dry-run protector** or water-level logic so the motor stops when the tank is full or the bore runs dry.
- Match **motor HP and stage count** to depth and yield (local pump dealer can advise).
- Install an **auto-cut / timer** so the pump does not run after the tank is full — reduces heat and electricity waste.
- Log **run hours**; sudden increase often means leakage, stuck float, or failed valve.

## When to call a technician

- Burning smell, smoke, or tripped breaker that will not reset after cooling.
- Repeated overheating after water flow is confirmed.
- 3-phase motor humming but not rotating properly.
- Any work inside the **control panel, starter, or capacitor**.

## Eshwar recommendation

For households and small buildings, an **Eshwar auto-cut timer** paired with tank-level logic helps stop the pump once the tank is full — cutting overheating risk from long dry or overflow cycles and lowering electricity use. Ask your dealer for the setup suited to your tank size and single/three-phase supply.

## Safety

Never open the motor or electrical panel yourself if you are not trained. Do not pour water on a hot motor to cool it. Children should stay away from pump rooms and open tanks.
